# Private online races

Implemented 30 September 2026 using the existing Supabase project and static native JavaScript app.

## Player flow

Open Challenge and sign in to host. Enter a race name and either an existing account email or leave the email blank for a guest link. Account invitations appear in the recipient’s Challenge inbox, refreshed every ten seconds while open; no email is sent. The response does not reveal whether an email belongs to an account. Guest links admit one opponent without requiring a Supabase anonymous-auth session.

Both players ready up. The server schedules a shared five-second countdown and a race lasting up to sixty seconds. Each tab opens typing when its countdown ends; the service marks the room racing on the first request after the start time, so no tab waits for a poll to say so. Completing the passage correctly can finish a player early. Progress is exchanged about every 650 ms, outside the local keystroke/render path. The server computes matching retained characters, elapsed race time and WPM; accuracy uses bounded insertion/error counters. Results remain friendly and unranked: browser data cannot establish that a human typed it. Quick match (below) is the one rated race, behind a trace check that is a floor, not anti-cheat.

The invitation expires fifteen minutes after room creation. Leaving cancels an active room for both players. A disconnected player’s seat remains held; the client retries automatically. Reload restores the last server-acknowledged text and counters. A ten-second final-packet grace period keeps elapsed time capped at sixty seconds and allows delayed results to arrive. A player who does not return is finalized from their last acknowledged progress. A new race requires a new room.

One keyboard retains the original local challenge. Online races do not add attempts, personal bests or learning comparisons. Revealed quote IDs do enter seen-text tracking so they cannot later masquerade as fresh retests.

## Friends, rematches and the inbox (7 October 2026)

- **Friends.** Each signed-in host keeps a private friend list on the race service (`race_private.friends`). Inviting an account by email saves that email to the list by default ("save to friends"). After a race against another account, "Add <name> to friends" saves them without ever revealing their email or account id; the row only says "raced before". The host who invited someone by email is not offered this, since they already have them by email. Challenge on a friend row creates an account invitation exactly as typing the email would, with the same non-revealing reply. Lists are capped at 100 and are not mutual: adding someone tells them nothing.
- **Race again.** Replaced on 27 October 2026 by Rematch on the results (below). Rooms opened by the first ask before then are still joined by the second player's ask.
- **Toast and inbox.** Signed-in players get an envelope next to the account button. Invitations are polled every ten seconds on every screen (paused while the tab is hidden or a run is in progress). A new invitation or rematch request shows a toast at the top right that leaves after 18 seconds, held while hovered or focused; a toast that arrives mid-run waits until the run ends. The inbox lists invitations and rematch requests (with Join), an opponent arriving while you are on another screen (with Open) and race results (with Race again). Open only works while that race is still live in this tab, and Race again lapses 23 hours after the room's expiry, before the service deletes the room; lapsed entries stay listed as closed. Entries are kept per user on this device only (`stilltype-inbox-v1:<user id>`, newest 30) and removed on sign-out. Guests see rematch toasts but keep no inbox.

The migration is `supabase/migrations/20261007031500_race_friends_rematch.sql`; `supabase/tests/race-friends-rematch.sql` covers friend privacy, friend invites, rematch seats and guest rematches. Both SQL test files pass against the two migrations on PostgreSQL 16 with a minimal `auth` / `extensions` stub. Apply the migration before shipping the frontend: until then friends and Race again report that they are being set up.

## Rematch, tally and the race replay (27 October 2026)

- **Results.** A finished race shows you on the left and the opponent on the right, each with their name, wpm and accuracy, and the pair's tally between them. The winner (more wpm, then more accuracy) reads larger and in full text colour, the other smaller and muted; a tie shows both at full size. The tally figure this race raised is the accent. The progress rows, "X leads.", New race and "Not counted in your progress." are gone from a finished race; Close race stays in the heading.
- **Rematch.** Each racer has a Rematch control under their own result: yours is a button, theirs shows what they picked (a ring, or a check once picked). Pressed again, a pick is taken back. When both have picked, the service opens the next room for the same seats, names, host and settings with a fresh passage, already counting down (no Ready), and both tabs move to it; nobody accepts anything, and no toast or inbox entry is involved. "Starting" shows under the tally until the tab has moved. A racer who closes a finished race, or goes on to another race from it, is marked as left: their side reads "left" and the other's button is inactive. The service charges the room to the racer who picked second. A finished race's results are polled every second while on screen and every three seconds otherwise. The inbox's Race again on a result picks Rematch on that race.
- **Tally.** `race_private.head_to_head` keeps one row per pair of accounts (the two ids in order, and each one's wins). It is updated in the transaction that finishes the race (`race_private.settle`, from the last finish or the grace period), so no browser writes a win; a tie adds nothing. Row-level security lets only the two accounts read their row, and browsers have no write privilege; the snapshot of a finished race carries it as `tally: [yours, theirs]`. Guests have no tally, and a race with a guest adds nothing. The row goes with either account.
- **Traces.** A racer's finishing progress carries the run's compact keystroke trace (the ghost challenge format, on the race clock from the shared start). The service keeps it on the player row only if it replays (`race_private.trace_score`) to the correct characters it settled, once, and drops anything else without failing the progress; a trace can also come with a later progress after the racer finished. A race resumed after a reload sends none. `traces` returns both once the race is finished, to its two seats only. Under the results, Graph (the default) draws both racers' speed on one chart in the style of the run card's (yours in the accent, theirs in the text colour), and Replay plays the passage with your spring caret and their ghost caret in the text colour, each named under it, with Play, 1× and Restart. Without both traces (a race from before this, or one resumed) neither shows.
- **Start.** A tab opens typing when its countdown ends, without waiting for a poll to report the room as racing.

The migration is `supabase/migrations/20261027120000_rematch_tally_traces.sql`; `supabase/tests/race-tally-traces.sql` covers the tally's privileges and row-level security, settling once on the last finish and on the grace period, ties, accuracy breaking a wpm tie, guests, traces kept, dropped, sent late and never replaced, who can read them, and account deletion. `supabase/tests/race-friends-rematch.sql` and `supabase/tests/account-deletion.sql` cover picks, taking one back, both picks opening the room counting down, the charge, a racer who left, and guests. Every SQL test file passes against all the migrations on a local Supabase stack (CLI 2.122.0, Postgres 17). The same migration moves the account-deletion trigger that forgets a deleted account's quota key to before the delete: after it, deleting an account that had asked for a rematch of a race it was invited to failed on the rematch room's foreign key. The frontend works before the migration (Rematch then opens or joins a room as before, and the tally, Graph and Replay stay hidden), so it can ship first.

## Race options (27 October 2026)

- **Challenge.** The Challenge page's button reads Challenge (it was Create race), and a friend row's Challenge works the same way. Plain Challenge races the host's practice setup (`raceOptions` in `src/race-options.js`): time, words or quote, the length (a custom one too), punctuation, and for generated text numbers and the word set. Adaptive, a drill, compose, custom text, book, code and the standard test are practice's own, so with any of them set the race is today's: a medium quote on a 60-second clock. Correction stays stop on error.
- **Customize.** Customize opens the practice row's own controls under the buttons (the mode and length segmented controls and the punctuation switch, cloned from the practice row, so they look and behave alike), starting from the practice setup. What it holds sets the next race only and never changes practice; entering a race closes it and drops its choices. Quote has no length or punctuation to pick.
- **Same race for both.** The host's browser makes the one passage (`racePassage`; a timed passage holds 400 wpm for its clock) and sends it with the conditions, so both racers type the stored text and no shared seed is needed. The race clock is `conditions.duration`: a timed race's own length, and two minutes as the limit on a word or quote race. A rematch keeps the race's conditions and makes a fresh passage for them.

The migration is `supabase/migrations/20261027130000_race_options.sql`, after `20261027120000_rematch_tally_traces.sql`. It lets a room's clock be 5 to 600 seconds, takes it from the conditions (60 without one), and has `race_action` end, cap and settle a race on its own clock. `supabase/tests/race-options.sql` covers refused clocks, the 60-second default, a timed finish, the grace period and the rematch's clock. Before it is applied, a race keeps the 60-second clock whatever its options, so a timed race other than 60 seconds runs a minute.

## Ghost races (9 October 2026)

- **Sending.** Under a finished, uninterrupted practice, retest or daily run, a signed-in user sees "Challenge a friend with this run". They pick someone from their friend list and the name the friend will see, then send. The run goes as its text, its test conditions and a compact keystroke trace: `{"v":1,"k":keys,"t":[ms since the previous key],"e":end ms}`, with one printable character per insertion and U+0008 per deletion (blocked keys change nothing and are left out). A slip onto a character a passage can't contain, such as ö or a no-break space, is sent as an ASCII stand-in that never matches the letter expected there, so the replay scores it the same. An emoji fills two places in the replay, so a run with one is not offered, and a race reply with one says why it wasn't sent. A word, quote or daily run sends its whole passage; a timed run's passage is cut at a word boundary past the sender's furthest key, with room for 400 wpm over the same clock. Drills, ghost race replies and runs that lost focus or used IME are never offered. A friend saved by email is resolved to an account when the challenge is sent; as with typed invitations, the reply does not say whether the email has one.
- **Racing.** The friend gets an inbox entry (and a toast, held during a run) with Race. The race uses the practice surface and the sender's conditions, so the same finish rule applies: a word, quote or daily passage ends at its end, a timed run on its clock. Esc restarts. The first finish without a break is sent; an interrupted try shows "Interrupted, so not sent." with Race again. - **Result.** The lead goes to more correct characters kept per minute. Under stop on error both runs end on the exact text, so that is the earlier finish; under continue on error a quick finish full of mistakes doesn't beat a clean one; a timed race compares the correct characters kept on the same clock. Both wpm are always shown. The friend's results view shows the two rows and the rule; both inboxes get one line such as "Lin raced your 25-word run: Lin finished first · you 72 wpm, Lin 65 wpm." ("typed faster" when the leader ended later, "typed further" on a timed run). The sender hears it by toast on the next inbox poll. A result is delivered once per device: clearing the inbox does not bring it back. The inbox keeps every open ghost race and every result from the last week however many there are, and the newest thirty other entries; a result older than a week that this device never showed stays out of it.
- **Scoring and limits.** The service replays each trace with metric v1 (`race_private.trace_score`, mirrored by `scoreTrace` in `src/ghost-race.js`; both work in double precision and round half up like `Math.round`, and both tests pin the same numbers, including half-way cases) and stores only the scores for the reply. It refuses unreadable traces, more than 6,000 keys or 64 KB, more than a hundred keys a second, an unfinished passage, a timed run off its clock, text outside 10–4,000 printable ASCII characters, conditions with any key or value the app doesn't send (a timed run must say so, on its own clock), and a recipient not on the sender's own list. The recipient's app checks a received row's conditions the same way (`challengeConditions`) and refuses to race one it can't set up again rather than trust it. The first reply stands: finishing again (a retry after a lost reply) returns the stored result and changes nothing. The browser makes one request id per run and friend and repeats it on a retry, so a send whose reply was lost is stored once and counts once. Each sender may send 20 challenges an hour and keep 30 waiting. Challenges expire seven days after sending; rows are deleted a month after expiry, opportunistically when anyone sends one. A trace is still browser data, so results stay friendly and unranked.
- **Kind.** A ghost race run is saved as kind `challenge`, which `src/insights.js` lists as non-comparable, as custom text is: never a personal best, never in Progress trends, comparable retests, recurring targets or recommendation evidence. Only its summary record is kept, on the 50-run budget for non-comparable runs, with no keystroke trace and no pair patterns, so ghost races never push practice traces out of the 24-trace budget or practice runs out of the 120-run pattern window. The trace sent to the service is built from the finished run in memory, and Send again reuses it. The footer reads "Not counted" during a ghost race, and its results offer no drill or retest. Its text id is worked out from the text (a corpus quote's own id, a daily text's id, or the id of exactly that text), never taken from the sender, and is marked seen, so a quote raced here cannot later count as fresh. Starting one ends a drill-and-retest cycle in progress, as the daily does. Practice-time features (a daily goal, a break notice) could reasonably count it, since it is real uninterrupted typing; none exists on master yet.
- **Storage and access.** `race_private.challenges` holds the sender, the recipient (null for an email with no account), both names (the sender's label for the friend is never shown to the friend), the text, conditions, clock, the sender's trace and score, the reply's score, `created_at`, `expires_at` and `completed_at`. Row-level security limits direct reads to the two parties, and the column grant leaves out both account ids and the sender's label; browsers have no write privileges. All access goes through `public.challenge_action` (signed-in accounts only), an invoker wrapper over a definer function, whose views never include account ids and include a passage and trace only for one party's `get`. Its inbox list returns every open challenge and every result from the last seven days, however many newer rows there are, then the newest fifty others.

The migration is `supabase/migrations/20261009100000_ghost_challenges.sql`; `supabase/tests/ghost-challenges.sql` covers privileges, RLS for each party and outsiders, trace scoring, conditions, friend-only recipients, email friends, timed runs, single replies, repeated sends and finishes, expiry, cleanup, quotas and the inbox list. It passes with the existing SQL tests against all three race migrations on PostgreSQL 16 with a minimal `auth` / `extensions` stub. **It has not been applied to the project.** Apply it before shipping the frontend; until then sending reports that ghost races are being set up, and the inbox carries on without them.

All four SQL test files pass against the six migrations on PostgreSQL 16 with the minimal stub, calling as `authenticated` with the JWT claim (and `anon`).

## Quick match and the race rating (10 November 2026)

- **Quick match.** Challenge's Quick match (`#quick-match`, the button reads Find a race) pairs a signed-in player with another waiting player at a close race rating, on the standard setup: 30 seconds of the 200 words, no punctuation or numbers, stop on error. The pair gets an ordinary race room, counting down at once, with the shared countdown, live progress, results, Graph and Replay, Rematch and the tally. The status line reads "Matched with Lin." during the countdown and "Leaving now counts as a loss." while racing, or "Lin left the race. Finish your run to win." once the other racer has left. The rating line under the control reads "Race rating 1520 · 1 rated race · provisional", and from 10 rated races leads with the rank, "Gale III · 1840 · 23 rated races". Guests see the control, and pressing it opens sign-in.
- **Nobody waiting.** After 20 seconds the status says nobody else is looking, with Keep waiting and Race your best run. The second races this device's fastest recorded run on the standard setup with the ghost caret, as a ghost race whose description says the ghost is your own recording. It leaves the queue and sends nothing.
- **What a stranger sees.** Only the race name ("Racer" when the field still holds the name taken from the email), the race and its results. The first press on a device says so once.
- **Rating.** Elo, 1500 to start, K 40 for the first 10 rated races and 20 after. Once a quick match starts, a seat finishes only with its own finishing packet, which carries its keystroke trace, and counts only when that trace passes the trace check; progress never decides it. Both count, and the traces decide. One counts, and it wins, rated, against a racer who dropped, reloaded or sent a run that couldn't be checked. Neither counts: no winner, unrated. Leave race after the start, before finishing, is a rated loss at once; the other racer races on alone and wins, rated, only with a checked run. Between the same two accounts only three rated results in 24 hours count, after which both read "Not rated: you and Lin have raced each other too often today.", and pairing prefers anyone else in range. Both result cards, the tally and the rating line read the same settled result, so a run that couldn't be checked is never marked the winner. The race closes ten seconds after its clock, settled by whichever request comes first: the staying racer's own poll, anyone's quick match request, or anyone opening a race. The results say how it moved and why, "Rated win: Lin didn’t finish. Race rating 1520 · 1 rated race · provisional (+20)", "Rated loss: your finish didn’t arrive before the race closed. …" or "Not rated: neither racer finished with a run that could be checked. …". A rated race that moves a ranked rating to another rank or step ends its line with where it came from, "Rated win. Gale III · 1808 · 23 rated races (+16), up from Gust I.", or "…, down from Gale III."; the race that ends the provisional period doesn't. A racer who closed the page reads the same line under Quick match when they come back.
- **Rank card.** A rated result shows a card (`#online-rank-card`, `src/rank-card.js`) built from one model, `ratingResult` in `src/quick-match.js`. It reads "Rank up", "Rated win", "Rated loss" or "Rated tie", then the rank's emblem (a hexagon holding the rank's mark and step), its name, "promoted from Gust I" or "down from Gale III", the rating counting from where it stood to where it settled, and the change, green for a gain and coral for a loss. One bar shows the 100-point step: a gain is laid on the end of the fill in white (near-black on the paper card), a loss pulls the fill back and leaves the lost stretch in coral. Under it, the step on the left and the distance to the next line on the right, "95 to Gale II". A promotion fills the old step to its end, then opens the new one from empty; a demotion empties the old step, then opens the lower one nearly full. Whisper's bar runs from 900 to 1200; Hurricane's is always full, with nothing on the right. Why a race ended, when the traces didn't simply decide it, is the card's last line. A provisional result's card shows only the rating, the change and "4 rated races · provisional"; an unrated result stays the sentence. Soft wind drifts behind the card, denser and faster up the ranks, left to right on a gain and back on a loss, and kept clear of every line of text; it runs only while the card is on screen and the tab is showing, and stops when the race closes. A light theme gets a paper card, a dark theme the dark one; the ranks' colours are the card's own and never follow the theme's accent, and `tests/rank-card.test.js` holds every pair to 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for the marks and bar. Under reduced motion the card appears in its final state with still wind. The card is hidden from assistive technology: the status region keeps the full sentence, which the card's text never contradicts. The rules, the grace, the thresholds and their reasons are in [ADR 0014](adr/0014-quick-match-and-race-rating.md), and the ranks in [ADR 0016](adr/0016-race-rank.md). The check is a floor, not anti-cheat.
- **Friends.** Both racers are offered "Add <name> to friends" after a quick match, the seat that was waiting included.

## The people you race (11 November 2026)

Challenge is one list of the people you race (TOB-420): a saved friend, someone who invited you, someone whose ghost is waiting. A person appears once: an invitation or ghost challenge the service links to a friend row is that friend's row; one it doesn't link is a row of its own, since nothing says who else it could be. Rows state only what the service holds: the open invitation and its age, a received ghost's speed and the time left, a finished ghost race's two speeds and its date, and the pair's tally for a friend saved from a race. There is no presence and no record of when two people last raced online, so neither is shown. Dismiss hides an invitation on this device only; the service has no decline.

The migration is `supabase/migrations/20261111120000_race_people.sql`. It stores nothing and changes no existing function: it adds `public.race_people` (signed-in accounts only), which reads `race_private.people` for the caller. That returns each friend row that holds an account with the pair's `head_to_head` counts, the caller's first, and the friend row of each open invitation (exactly those `race_action`'s `invites` lists) and each ghost challenge whose other party is an account on the caller's list. Friends saved by typed email are never matched to an account, so the reply never says whether an email belongs to one. `supabase/tests/race-people.sql` covers privileges, guests and `anon`, an empty list, no tally before a race and each side's own order after, an email friend whose account has a tally staying unlinked, an invitation from a friend linked and one from an email friend not, a joined invitation dropping out, ghost challenges either way linked and another pair's never shown, an outsider seeing nothing, removing a friend, and account deletion. Every SQL test file passes against all the migrations on PostgreSQL 17 (the `supabase/postgres` 17.11 image) with the minimal `auth` stub (`email_confirmed_at` on `auth.users`). The frontend works without it: until it is applied, rows have no tally and invitations and ghosts are rows of their own. A missing `race_people` is asked for once per page load (one 404 and its console line), never again until a reload.

## Storage and access

- `src/multiplayer.js` calls `public.race_action` using the existing browser publishable key and a member token when signed in. It refreshes sessions and maintains progress sequence numbers across reloads.
- `race_private.rooms` and `race_private.players` enable RLS and grant browsers no table privileges. An explicitly granted invoker RPC calls a private definer function that checks account membership or an independent guest-seat capability on every operation. Transitions lock the room row; hosting quotas serialize by account. A rematch counts against the racer who asks for it (their account, or a guest's seat), not the original host.
- Account lookups stay inside the database. Snapshots omit account IDs, emails, capability hashes and the other player’s typed text/counters. Both players can see names, readiness, progress and scores.
- A guest invitation and the joined guest’s seat use different random 256-bit capabilities. Only hashes are stored in the database. Invitation capabilities live in URL fragments; joined-seat credentials and the host’s link are retained only in the current tab’s sessionStorage, along with any pending invite needed through sign-in redirects. Solo guest history is still not persisted.
- Room data includes current typed text, counters and scores. Creating a room opportunistically removes rooms whose expiration was more than a day ago; there is no scheduled deletion guarantee while the app is idle.
- Deleting an account (`supabase/functions/delete-account`) removes its race rows through the foreign keys to `auth.users`: rooms it hosts or was invited to, its seats in other players' rooms, its friend list and its place on other lists as a raced friend. An email someone else typed into their own friend list stays there and no longer matches an account. A rematch room it opened in someone else's race stays, as that racer's room; a trigger (`supabase/migrations/20261009140000_forget_deleted_race_opener.sql`) replaces the deleted account's quota key (`opened_by`) with `deleted`.

The migration is `supabase/migrations/20260930085227_online_races.sql`, applied to project `bicbyljexprkaedakofa` (`typingsite`) as remote migration `20260930085736`. No new runtime dependency or WebSocket server is required. The static frontend build uses the existing hosting workflow; this change does not publish a frontend deployment.
