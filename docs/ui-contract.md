# UI contract

This document describes the behavior shared by the React screens and native typing hosts. `src/app.tsx` owns the screen tree; typed stores publish cached views. Native input, passage geometry and animation retain their own ref subtrees. The app remains one document with a shared typing surface, so practice, targeted drills and fresh-text retests keep the same geometry and input behavior.

## Screen model

- `body[data-view]` is one of `practice`, `results`, `train`, `progress`, `compete`, or `learn`. `learn` has no dock item; it opens from `[data-action="open-lessons"]` (the quiet "learn to type" link in the practice controls and the first-run links) and the palette.
- Only the matching `[data-screen]` is visible. Navigation controls use `[data-nav]` with the same values and set `aria-current="page"` (and `.is-active`) on the active item. The navigation labels them practice, train, progress and challenge; the brand link also navigates to practice.
- `#typing-screen` is the shared typing surface. Its `data-session` value is one of `practice`, `drill`, `retest`, `daily`, or `lesson`; `body[data-session]` mirrors it for styling.
- Train, progress, and challenge use `#train-screen`, `#progress-screen`, and `#compete-screen`. Challenge's views are described under Challenge below.
- `body[data-running]` is present from the first keystroke of a run until it finishes or restarts. While it is set, chrome other than the passage and its measure row fades out; pointer movement, finishing or restarting brings it back. It never changes the passage geometry.
- Buttons with `[data-action]` expose intent for delegated event handlers. All navigation and state changes happen without a page reload.

## Theme

`html[data-theme]` is `dark` or `light`. An inline script in `<head>` resolves it before first paint from `preferences.theme` in the `stilltype-profile-v2` profile, falling back to `prefers-color-scheme`. The stored preference is `system`, `dark` or `light`; `system` follows the OS and updates when it changes. `meta[name="theme-color"]` is kept in step with the resolved theme.

## Quote filters

Quote filters use the select-only comboboxes `#quote-length` (labelled "length") and `#quote-difficulty` in `#quote-options`. Each label, current value and chevron is one 44px target with natural text width. Their opaque absolute listboxes do not change the rail layout. Arrow keys, Home, End and typeahead explore a draft; Enter or Space commits and focuses practice. Escape or the same trigger cancels. Tab, Shift-Tab and outside clicks commit without moving focus away from the destination. Selecting the current value closes without restarting. Only one quote or more menu opens at a time. Book mode's `#book-options` uses the same combobox, `#book-select` (labelled "book", the label visually hidden), followed by `#book-page` ("page N"). Preference refreshes, mode/view/focus changes, resize, window blur and commands dismiss the draft. Settings selects remain native.

## Frame, settings and command palette

The frame holds the navigation (`nav.dock`, named "Main navigation"): the brand link, `#side-bar-toggle`, the `.dock-nav` view buttons, `#palette-trigger` ("commands", with its key) and `#settings-trigger`, which opens `dialog#settings-dialog`. Beside it, `.corner` holds `#inbox` and `#account`, `details` whose `.corner-popover` opens from their triggers. `html[data-navigation]` is `side` or `top` (`preferences.navigation`, a display preference, default `side`); CSS alone turns it and the width into one of three layouts:

- **Side bar.** From 960px with `side`: `#side-bar` (`.dock-zone`) is a fixed column at the left with a hairline edge, holding the navigation as a vertical list, then at its foot the corner and the footer. On a short window the list scrolls inside the bar and the foot stays in reach. Its popovers open to its right. The page sits beside it.
- **Top bar.** With `top`, or from 761px to 959px either way: the navigation is a flat bar across the top with the corner at its end, and the footer sits at the foot of the page. `#side-bar` has no box of its own (`display: contents`).
- **Phone.** Under 761px either way: the navigation is a flat bar fixed to the bottom with the short labels, and the corner sits at the top right.

The footer (`.site-footer`) renders once, where the layout shows it, so the tab order follows the eye: inside `#side-bar` in the side bar layout (`side` and at least 960px wide, read from a `(min-width: 960px)` listener), otherwise after `<main>`. `#side-bar-toggle` (`aria-controls="side-bar"`, `aria-expanded`, "Hide side bar" or "Show side bar") and the palette's "hide side bar" and "show side bar" slide the side bar out to the left and back by transform, while the page glides from its old place; neither moves under reduced motion. Hidden, the bar is out of the tab order and the accessibility tree, and the toggle waits at the top left of the page. That is `html[data-side-bar]` (`shown` or `hidden`): device state, never synced, kept per signed-in person in `localStorage` (`stilltype-side-bar-v1:<user id>`) and restored before first paint, followed by that person's other open tabs as it changes, and kept for the tab only for a guest. It means nothing outside the side bar layout. `src/prepaint.ts` applies both attributes before first paint. Restart (`#restart-button`) stays in the typing footer.

The collapsed `#test-content-settings` category contains `#word-list`, `#numbers-toggle` and `#repeat-quotes-toggle`. Numbers default off, apply only to time/word tests, and remain available through the "numbers" command. They are recorded in run conditions, keeping bests and retests separate from runs without numbers. Quote and daily passages ignore the preference. The passage mode bar has no numbers control.

Selection pills: a `.glide` container with `data-glide="current"` follows `aria-current`; `data-glide="pressed"` follows `aria-pressed`. Its `.glide-pill` is aria-hidden decoration; state always lives in the ARIA attributes and `.is-active`.

Settings are `#theme-select` (`system`, `dark`, `light`), `#navigation-select` (`side`, `top`), `#font-size` (`22`, `28`, `36`), `#typing-font` (`literata`, `source-serif`, `inter`, `jetbrains-mono`, `ibm-plex-mono`, `roboto-mono`, `fira-code`), `#app-font` (`literata`, `source-serif`, `inter`), `#speed-unit` (`wpm`, `cpm`), `#word-highlight` (`off`, `current`, `next`), `#word-list`, `#repeat-quotes-toggle` (both in the test content category), `#live-toggle`, `#keyboard-toggle`, `#tape-toggle`, `#correction-select`, `#min-accuracy` (`0` off, `80`, `90`, `95`, `98`) and `#min-wpm` (`0` off, `20`–`100`) sharing one `minimums` row, `#focus-toggle`, `#train-focus` (`balanced`, `accuracy`, `speed`), `#drill-length` (`15`, `30`, `60`, `120`) and `#retest-toggle` (`true`, `false`), `#sound-select` (`off`, `clicks`, `clicks-errors`) and `#sound-volume` (a 0–100 range in `#sound-volume-row`, disabled while sound is off, with its value in `#sound-volume-value`); `#exit-focus` sits outside the dock. While a minimum is set, `#minimum-note` names it on its own line under the practice controls; it is hidden while Custom or composing is open, a custom passage is loaded or the standard test is selected, since those runs have no minimums and run to the end. Apply theme as `html[data-theme]`; navigation as `html[data-navigation]`; font size as `body[data-font-size]`; word highlight as `body[data-word-highlight]`; the one-line tape as `body[data-tape]` (the layout on screen: `false` during a ghost race, which keeps three lines whatever `#tape-toggle` says); live metrics as `body[data-live-metrics]`; keyboard as `body[data-keyboard]`; focus mode as `body[data-focus-mode]`. `#correction-select` picks the correction policy (`CORRECTION_POLICIES` in `src/typing.js`): `relaxed` ("free", the default: mistakes can be typed through and fixed), `mandatory` ("stop on error": the caret waits on a wrong letter), `no-backspace` (mistakes stay; backspace does nothing), `fail-word` (a word left wrong at a space or the end fails the run) and `fail-any` (any wrong key fails the run). Profiles that stored the old boolean `stopOnError` map to `mandatory` or `relaxed`. Changing the correction policy starts a new practice run. The policy is stored in run conditions, keeping bests and comparisons separate. Repeat quotes (`preferences.repeatQuotes`, off by default; also the palette command "repeat quotes"; both disabled outside quote mode) lets open quote practice draw a quote the profile has already seen. Turning it off replaces an unstarted repeated passage with unseen text, at once on Practice and otherwise when Practice next opens. Such a passage is `fresh: false`: `#input-help` says so before and during the run, and the result is labelled repeated text and compared only with earlier runs on the same passage. Repeated text never sets a best, enters trends, weekly medians or pattern evidence, or becomes either side of a retest; retests always draw unseen text. The speed unit is display only: every speed is saved, scored, compared and keyed in wpm, and shown in the chosen unit (cpm = wpm × 5, `src/units.js`). Each speed figure the app writes is a `<data>` whose `value` and `data-wpm` hold the wpm, and unit labels carry `data-speed-unit` (empty for the bare unit, otherwise a `{unit}` template). The raw speed tooltip (`#result-raw-row`) is worded for the chosen unit. Speeds a person sets are shown and entered in the chosen unit and saved in wpm: `#min-wpm` option labels, the palette's minimum speed commands, and `#pace-wpm` (its value, bounds, step and `#pace-range`, all in the unit in its `data-unit`, which an unsaved pace being typed keeps until the field is left; a saved pace, Enter included, reads back at once as the figure saved; a typed cpm is saved as the nearest whole wpm). A minimum speed reads in the chosen unit wherever it is named (conditions line, run card note, Progress detail); changing the unit redraws the figures already on screen (toasts included) without starting a new run, closing open Progress rows or moving focus. A figure inside a showing live region may be read out again in the new unit; that is accepted, since a unit change is almost always the person's own and one synced from another device mid-session is rare. A synced change held back through a run applies as it ends, and never stops that run's result being written to its live region. The generated keyboard belongs in `#keyboard`. The default Escape binding exits focus mode before restarting on a later press. Strict space (off by default) refuses a space where the passage has a letter, scoring it as a wrong key without moving the caret and tinting the skipped letter until the next key. Quick end (on by default) ends a continue-on-error word or quote run on its last letter; off, a mistake in the last word waits for a fix or a space. `#word-flow-toggle` (`fluid`, the default, or `linear`, which an account whose saved runs are all linear and that never chose keeps; also the palette's "word flow" commands) sets how a free-correction practice run takes a space mid-word, letters past a word's end and Backspace at a word's start ([ADR 0027](adr/0027-fluid-word-flow-and-spring-caret.md)); races, the standard test, lessons, code, the daily passage, strict space and the other correction policies stay linear. Changing it starts a new practice run. What a session can take comes from that session (its kind, timing and run conditions), not the test-shape preferences: `#quick-end-toggle` is disabled, keeping its value, wherever quick end can't change the run (timed runs, including every drill, and stop on error), and `#quick-end-note` says why. A change on the practice screen restarts the session as the same kind; the daily and a retest keep the rules they started with, carry on, and `#rules-note` (read by both switches) says so. In Challenge's One keyboard view the session is the race, an untimed quote whatever the practice shape: the switches act on the next race, and a race under way keeps its rules for both players and says so. Starting a retest or drill restores its baseline's test shape but never writes its rules into these switches: a retest takes them for its own conditions, and the person's choice waits for the next run it applies to. Other screens stay put, and a practice passage left waiting there is rebuilt when practice is shown again. A drill or retest nobody has started stays waiting when Practice is shown as it stands from another screen (the dock, the palette's "practice", focus mode); Practice pressed on Practice leaves it for practice, and everything that asks for a test (a mode or length, custom text, a word list, punctuation, numbers, a layout, the daily passage, a lesson, missed words, a test setting in Settings) starts that instead. A clear here or elsewhere replaces a waiting retest, whose baseline it took, with a practice test; a waiting drill stands alone. A daily passage, a lesson and missed-word practice nobody has started give way to a practice test when Practice is opened from another screen (`e2e/practice-return.spec.js`). Both enter run conditions only when they differ from the defaults and apply to the run. `#layout-select` (`qwerty`, `dvorak`, `colemak`, `colemak-dh`, `workman`; from `src/layouts.js`) sets the keyboard legend and is recorded as `layout` in run conditions; changing it starts a new practice run. Typed characters are never remapped. `#finger-toggle` sits in `#finger-row`, which is shown only while the keyboard is on; below 760px both keyboard rows are hidden with the keyboard. `#keyboard` carries `data-layout` and `data-fingers`; each `.key` carries `data-key`, `data-hand` and `data-finger`; the finger map draws each finger's initial as aria-hidden generated text, gives each key one spoken phrase naming its hand and finger (an aria-hidden printed cap plus `.sr-only` text such as "f, left index"; keys are plain letters while the map is off) and adds a `.finger-legend`. Retests and drills never change the layout; a physical-keyboard baseline typed on another layout is refused for retest. Touch runs record the default layout and are never refused for it.


The configurable exit-focus shortcut or `#exit-focus` exits focus mode; Escape does so by default.

The command palette is `dialog#palette`. `#palette-input` is a combobox controlling `#palette-list` (a listbox rebuilt from the app's commands on every keystroke, so hints show current state); `#palette-empty` shows when nothing matches; `.palette-highlight` is aria-hidden. A query ranks commands in three tiers that never cross: those whose text starts with it, then those that hold it, then those that hold its letters in order. The commands binding (Ctrl-K / ⌘K by default) and `#palette-trigger` open it; Escape closes it and returns focus; the commands binding pressed while it fades out opens it again. Opening it closes settings, and opening it during a run marks that run interrupted.

## Shared typing surface

Required IDs: `typing-screen`, `typing-form`, `typing-input`, `typing-wrap`, `text-display`, `accessible-passage`, `live-stats`, `live-wpm`, `live-accuracy`, `timer`, `timer-unit`, `timer-label`, `progress-track`, `progress-bar`, `typing-status`, `session-kicker`, `session-title`, `session-description`, `condition-line`, `input-help`, `restart-button`, and `announcement`.

`#text-display` receives `.word > .char` spans. Character state classes are `.correct`, `.wrong`, `.missed` (a letter a space skipped, fluid word flow) and `.current`; they change instantly, without transition. Letters typed past a word's end in fluid word flow are `.char.extra` spans at the end of that `.word`, before its space, at most eight. `.wrong` always has a non-colour tint; on a space it is painted at full line height so the row keeps an even band. Under `forced-colors: active` it switches to the system `Mark` / `MarkText` highlight so the mark survives the forced palette. Every rendered passage (`#text-display`, `#race-text-display`, `#online-text-display`) also marks the `.word` holding the caret `.word-current` and the one after it `.word-next` (a space belongs to the word before it); `body[data-word-highlight]` decides which, if either, shows the `--word-highlight` tint. Like the letter states it changes instantly, without transition, and is never scored. Watch replay (`#replay-dialog`) and the composing field have no current word and never show it. The display has a fixed three-line height (one line with `body[data-tape="true"]`). Live metrics keep the display geometry fixed. `src/motion.ts` places the caret, ghost caret and sliding word highlight in the display. The caret moves between letters on the same row and jumps to the next row. Typing motion in Appearance (`#typing-motion`: `spring`, the default, carries the caret's speed from key to key and stops its frames at rest; `smooth` glides over `#caret-glide`, enabled only for it; `instant`) is applied as `body[data-typing-motion]`; instant makes the caret, passage scroll and word highlight move instantly. The system reduced-motion preference also makes these updates instant.

`#typing-input` is the real focus target and remains visually transparent over the displayed text. `#progress-track` is a thin line with `role="progressbar"`; keep `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax`, and `aria-valuetext` current. `#progress-bar` fills from its `--progress` custom property (0–1), not its width.

`#live-stats` is plain text and is not a live region; update it at a restrained cadence. It is a row of four `.cell`s: `#timer-label` names the first (time or words) over `#timer` and `#timer-unit`, then wpm (its unit word is the label, `[data-speed-unit]`), accuracy, and `#live-standing`, the average (`recentAverage`, the last ten counted fresh runs) and best at the conditions on screen, a dash for none. With `body[data-live-metrics=false]` wpm and accuracy keep their cells and show a dash. A phone shows the first three only. Completion is announced once through `#announcement`.

Practice mode controls are `#mode-time`, `#mode-words`, `#mode-quote`, `#duration-options`, `#punctuation-toggle`, `#quote-options`, `#quote-length`, and `#quote-difficulty`. The four preset length buttons in `#duration-options` carry `data-length`, are relabelled in place when the mode changes (never rebuilt) and use `aria-pressed`. `#custom-length`, the last pill, reads "custom" or the custom value in use; pressing it hides it and shows `form#custom-length-form` with `#custom-length-input` in its place (`.practice-controls[data-editing]` while open, which also shows the row in focus mode). Enter, or leaving the field with a valid value, applies it; Escape restores the pill; pressing another control in the row drops the edit. In focus mode, where the row hides again as the field closes, focus that would land in the row goes to `#typing-input`. Invalid input sets `aria-invalid` and a message in `#custom-length-error` (`role="alert"`), positioned in the gap under the row so the passage never moves; the message stays visible in focus mode and clears when the next run starts. The palette commands "custom time…" and "custom words…" open the same field.

Practice mode controls are `#mode-time`, `#mode-words`, `#mode-quote`, `#mode-standard`, `#mode-book`, `#duration-options`, `#punctuation-toggle`, `#quote-options`, `#quote-length`, and `#quote-difficulty`. Generated length buttons belong in `#duration-options` and use `aria-pressed`. `#mode-compose` sits in the same group (`data-compose`, not `data-mode`) and opens composing practice.

`#mode-standard` selects the standard test: one fixed, versioned recipe (`STANDARD_VERSION` in `src/typing.js`, now `standard-v1`) of 60 s, stop on error, sentences from the 1k/5k/10k word bands with punctuation and numbers, the second of them in quotation marks as the quote segment. Every word, the quote's included, comes from the attempt's own seed (`standardPassage`), recorded in the text ID, so no attempt repeats text from the quote corpus or an earlier attempt. On layouts where `"` is a dead key, browsers report composition, which isn't scored, so those typists need #61 (dead-key input) to complete a standard run. Its conditions carry `recipe`, which `conditionKey` appends only when present, so it has its own bests, trends and retests. While it is selected, `#standard-options` (with `#standard-recipe`) replaces the length buttons, and `#punctuation-toggle`, `#word-list`, `#numbers-toggle` and `#stop-on-error-toggle` are disabled with the recipe's values (numbers on) and, in settings, a `.preference-lock` note; the palette leaves out the punctuation, numbers and word list commands. Standard conditions carry no `numbers` field, since the recipe fixes them, so standard keys are unchanged by the numbers toggle. `#word-list` gains its recipe option (`standard`) only while standard is selected, and `setWordList` accepts only real list IDs; the person's own settings are kept for the other modes.

## Composing practice

`body[data-compose="true"]` while composing is open: `#typing-form` is hidden and `#compose-form` (`data-state` `ready` or `done`) takes its place on the practice screen; the length options and `#punctuation-switch` are hidden and the footer best is hidden. The form holds `#compose-prompt`, `#compose-conditions`, `#compose-another`, `#compose-timer`, the free-typing `#compose-input` (described by the prompt, `#compose-hint` and `#compose-privacy`), the `#compose-progress` progressbar, `#compose-help` for notices, `#compose-restart` and `#compose-done` (Ctrl/⌘+Enter). The result is `#compose-result`: `#compose-result-heading`, `#compose-wpm`, `#compose-backspace`, `#compose-active`, `#compose-characters`, the definition `#compose-definition`, `#compose-saved`, `#compose-retry` and `#compose-next`, which takes focus on finishing. Escape in the field starts over. Where `CSS.supports('caret-animation', 'manual')` is false, `src/compose-caret.js` sets `data-caret="drawn"` on `.compose-wrap`, which makes the field’s native caret transparent, and inserts one aria-hidden `.compose-caret-layer` after the field, holding an empty hidden mirror and the `.compose-caret` bar (no animation or transition, `hidden` unless the field is focused, writable and has a collapsed selection); otherwise it adds nothing. Choosing time, words or quote, a drill, a retest or the daily ends composing; settings and sync wait until it ends. Progress lists saved sessions in `#compose-history` (`#compose-history-note`, `#compose-history-list`), apart from the copy-test charts. Only numbers are stored, never the text.

`#best` in the footer shows the personal best for the conditions of the session on screen, or states that there is none: the daily's and a retest's own conditions (a retest's rules are its baseline's, whatever the switches say now), otherwise the current settings; custom text says it isn't counted. `#suggest-trigger` beside it (and the palette command "suggest a feature…") opens `dialog#suggest-dialog`: `#suggest-text` (max 1000 characters, counted in `#suggest-count`), errors in `#suggest-error`, a hidden honeypot `#suggest-website`, and `#send-suggest`, which inserts `{ body, view }` into the Supabase `feature_suggestions` table through `sendSuggestion` in `src/account.js`, always as the anonymous role (a filled honeypot sends nothing). Success closes the dialog and shows `#suggest-toast`; failure keeps the text. Opening it mid-run marks the run interrupted, like the palette.

## Completed run and recommendation

`#results-heading` contains `#result-delta`, the one-line headline for the run. `#result-comparison-note` stays empty except for interrupted-run and storage messages.

The run card is `#run-card`: `dl.run-card-facts`, four cells with a note each (`#result-wpm` with `#result-versus`, its change against the average before this run, absent with no earlier run; `#result-accuracy` with the words marked `.word-miss`; `#result-time` with the words typed; `#result-consistency` with `#result-range`, the trace's slowest to fastest point, absent under 3 seconds, a display figure never stored), then a speed panel holding `#speed-graph` (an SVG speed-over-time graph drawn by `src/graph.js` from the attempt's keystrokes, mistakes in coral; `role="img"` with a text summary in its `aria-label`; presentational only), the accuracy fact `#result-accuracy-fact` titled with first-try errors and the final c/i/e/m, a typed panel holding the words `#result-words` with `#result-word-detail` (the chosen word's pace, empty before a choice), `.result-actions` (Run again, the drill, Watch replay and `#result-conditions`) and, after a run that counts as evidence and has missed or timed words (a complete test, retest or daily run, by the same rule as recurring patterns; never a drill, a daily passage shown before, custom text or word practice itself), `#practice-words` (the missed words are the coral ones in `#result-words`; this run's slowest clean words join them), which starts an uncounted `missed` practice run of those words. That run becomes the baseline for the fresh retest offered afterward, which reads and records no drill target. The card is a static print: numbers are set directly, with no tilt, reels or count-up.

The palette command "practice words you keep missing" starts an uncounted `missed` run of the words missed on the first try in at least two of the newest counted runs (`recurringMissedWords` in `src/insights.js`: complete tests, retests and new daily passages, on the newest such run's input class). With fewer than two such words it starts nothing.

The manual target lives in `details.manual-panel` (summary `#manual-heading`): `#manual-target` form, `#manual-target-input`, and `#apply-manual-target`. It accepts a short letter pair or word. Validation appears in `#manual-target-error`; invalid state uses `aria-invalid="true"` and visible recovery copy.

## Train

Train is the queue and the chosen step (TOB-470), drawn from Train's path (TOB-420): `trainPath` in `src/train-path.js` builds the targets you have drilled, the one to drill now and the next ones the learner queues as a pure function of the profile's runs and loops, the learner's recommendation in the train focus (`recommendFor`) and, after a retest, the unit just retested: `{ behind, just, current, ahead }`. `trainFigures` in `src/train-cost.js` labels those steps with what each costs or won back, from the learner's own fit (`fitLearner`) and the runs. The page draws exactly what they return. The same profile and settings give the same page on any device, and after a merge in either order.

- **No visible heading.** The dock names the page. `#train-heading` is visually hidden (`.sr-only`, focusable with `tabindex="-1"`) and holds the open step's title ("Practice “th”", or "General drill"). A palette jump to Train focuses it.
- **The queue.** A panel, "queue" with a "cost" column. `ol#train-path` (accessible name "queue") holds one `li.queue-step` per step still to do: `#train-current` (`aria-current="step"`, the open step, its unit in `#train-unit`), then `.is-ahead`, the next three units in the queue. Each is one `button.q-row`: its number, the unit, a word for why it is queued (`.q-why`: "slow" or "missed", the basis the focus ranked it on), its cost (`.q-cost`) and, as its title, its evidence ("5 of 40 missed", "120 ms slower"). With nothing named the open step is the general drill: `#train-unit` reads "General drill", it has no cost, and nothing is queued after it.
- **Cost.** What a unit takes from the person's speed, an estimate: the wpm they would gain if it took no longer than any other letter. The learner ranks a unit by its gain (misses a letter typed above the person's typical rate, slowness counted in misses at the intervals a miss costs); at the person's pace (their median speed over their newest 10 measured runs on the keyboard) that is a loss of `lost` ms on every letter, and the cost is wpm at (pace − lost) − wpm at pace. The cost rises with the gain alone, so it never reorders the queue, and it reads the basis the focus ranked on. A cost below 0.1 reads "<0.1"; with no measured run, or a loss as large as a letter's time, there is none ("—"). It is shown in the speed unit on screen.
- **The fold.** Under the queue, while anything has been drilled, `button#train-drilled-toggle` ("drilled", the count, and the speed won back between them, `aria-expanded`) opens `ol#train-drilled`: one `li.queue-step.is-behind` per target with a loop on this keyboard, at its newest loop, oldest first, `.is-drilled` (a ring) or `.is-fixed` (a check), its state word (`.q-state`), what it won back, and its read-back as its title. A step won back its cost before the drill less its cost on the retest, by the same formula, from the loop's own read-back (misses above the typical rate, or ms past the pace); a loop too thin to read back has no figure. A drill with the retest off saves no loop, so it is never in the fold. The fold opens on its own after a retest.
- **Choosing a step.** Every row is a button; the chosen one is `aria-pressed="true"` and the detail beside the queue (under it up to 900px) shows it. Opening Train chooses the open step. A redraw keeps the step chosen and the fold open while the step is still there.
- **The chosen step's detail.** Its kind ("next · pair", "pair", "fixed · key"), the unit large, the first six words of its drill's text with the unit marked, and its button with `#train-starts`, the drill and retest lengths ("30 s + 10 words retest", or "30 s" when no retest follows). For a step to do, three cells: cost (≈ N in the speed unit), then slower (N ms, with the pair against the pace, "290 vs 200") for a unit queued on its pace or missed (N/M) for one queued on its misses, then frequency (N a minute at the person's speed, "1 in N letters", the unit's share of English from the learner's table). For a drilled step: won back, its misses or pace now (since the drill once more than its retest holds it, else the retest) and before, and its read-back lines. The general drill's detail has the drill's first words and its button only.
- **The open step's evidence.** `#train-next` holds `#train-evidence` (the learner's observed evidence, `evidenceCopy`: misses for a unit named on its misses, pace for a pair named on its pace; "across all N runs" once it has been drilled), `#train-last` (its "since you drilled it" line, before → since, when it has a loop, of the kind of evidence the step is drawn for, in counts and never a rate: "6 of 31 → 2 of 6 missed since you drilled it", or "80 ms slower → at your pace since you drilled it, over 3 intervals") and `#train-words` (below). It shows with the open step and with the comparison.
- **The button.** `#start-drill` drills the chosen step: for the open step "Drill “th”" for a named unit, "Drill and retest" for a general drill when a retest follows, and "Drill" when none does (the retest is off, or there is no baseline); for a step ahead "Drill “an”" and for a drilled step "Drill “an” again", which drill it through `practiceTarget`, so the loop records the policy and focus that queued it. After a retest with a named next unit the open step's button is `#comparison-next` ("Next: “an”"), which drills that unit through `practiceTarget`.
- **Drilled and fixed.** One retest makes a target drilled. A drilled target is fixed when the learner no longer queues it and at least two runs since its drill hold observations of the kind it was drilled for (first tries for a target drilled for its misses, clean intervals for a pair drilled for its pace), at least 20 of them between those runs, with no first-try miss among them, or a mean slowdown of 20 ms or less. Otherwise it is drilled. (`isFixed`, `FIXED_RUNS`, `FIXED_OBSERVATIONS`, `SLOW_FLOOR_MS`.) A run that holds the pair but no observation of that kind is not one of the two. A fixed target the learner names again leaves the fold for the queue. No unit is both drilled and queued.
- **After a retest.** The comparison is the newest step drilled (`#train-drilled > .is-just`, drawn from the comparison rather than a loop), chosen, with the fold open; the open step is the first unit queued after the one just retested. Its detail is `#comparison-panel`: `#comparison-unit` (the unit, or "General drill", "Adaptive", or "Retest" for a retest whose drill named no unit), `#comparison-status` (the outcome sentence, with a status dot coloured by `data-outcome`), its figures as cells, the conditions and the caveat, then the open step's button, `#comparison-back` and `#train-starts`, then the open step's evidence. `#comparison-status` takes focus; Tab then reaches the open step's button and `#comparison-back`. Up to 900px the detail is scrolled into view. Nothing moves as it opens. Opening Train again draws it afresh at your settings, the open step chosen.
- **Following the history.** Any change to the history this tab holds (a merge from another tab or device, a clear or its undo, here or elsewhere) goes through `followHistory`, which redraws every training surface on show through `showTraining`: Train, the results card's Drill, the comparison's Next, Progress's lead cells and what moved it, and the lessons' hand-off. A clear that removes the comparison's runs takes the comparison and its Next away, and Train opens on the cleared history. Mid-run nothing changes; the surfaces follow when the run ends. A redraw keeps keyboard focus on the row that had it, or gives it to the open step's button when that row is gone.

`#train-words` lists missed words that hold a target named for its misses, the target in the text colour and the rest muted, never coral (where in the word the miss fell isn't kept); a pair named for its pace lists none.

## Drill and retest

`#start-drill` changes the shared typing surface to `data-session="drill"`; the page uses the same input and text display with target-heavy generated text. The drill runs the length `#drill-length` set when it started (30 s by default; `#session-kicker` reads "drill · N s"). With `#retest-toggle` off, `#start-drill` reads "Drill", the drill ends on its own result card with nothing compared, and no loop is saved. The train focus, the drill length and the retest are preferences, never test conditions, and are also palette commands ("train focus · …", "drill length · N seconds", "retest after drill"). Every surface that shows what Train would drill (the run card's `#result-drill`, Train's queue with `#start-drill`, the comparison's `#comparison-next` and the steps ahead) asks the recommender again when one of them changes, here or by sync from another device, and its button drills what it shows; the loop records the focus that ranked it. An adaptive passage nobody has started is drawn again for a new focus: at once when Practice is showing, and otherwise on the way back to it. A plain passage is kept, so the plain test that follows an adaptive run stays the baseline it was drawn to be. The lessons' hand-off, which states the drill's length and whether a retest follows, is redrawn with them. A drill whose history another device cleared while it waited still runs, shows and records the length it was drawn with. If it fails, it keeps "Try drill again", which then asks the recommender afresh. A drill or retest under way keeps the target, length and retest setting it started with, so a change mid-drill applies to the next one. Drill completion changes it to `data-session="retest"`; the passage must be fresh and the condition line must say whether comparison is valid. A drill that a failure policy (`fail-word`, `fail-any`) ends does not move on by itself: it shows its failed result like any failed run, with the run card's `#result-drill` relabelled "Try drill again" and focused. Its loop then records the drill as not completed.

Retest outcome IDs are `#comparison-panel`, `#comparison-status`, `#comparison-target-row` (`#comparison-target-label`, `#comparison-target`, `#comparison-target-was`), `#comparison-accuracy-now`, `#comparison-accuracy`, `#comparison-wpm-now`, `#comparison-wpm`, `#comparison-conditions` and `#comparison-caveat`. ## Progress and local history

Progress answers one question first: what changed since last week, and what moved it (TOB-420). `weekChange` in `src/week-change.js` builds the overview as one value, a pure function of the profile's runs, loops and practice days, the chosen condition key, the train focus, the daily goal, the period and `now`: `{ lead, movers, practice, charts }`. It is drawn in the Instrument look (TOB-470): from the top, the conditions and the period, four lead cells, two chart panels, the practice calendar and the runs, each a `.panel` or `.cells` row.

## Accessibility and sizing

All controls are reachable by keyboard and use one focus ring (the accent outline). Mobile interactive targets are at least 44 px. The skip link targets `#typing-input`. Status updates use `#announcement`. IME composition, paste, touch-only input, and focus interruption are announced and displayed in `#input-help`; accent (dead) key compositions on `#typing-input` and `#race-input` are scored as the one or two characters they commit. A composition is an accent key's only if one opened it: its `Dead` keydown, before the mark shows or (Safari) just after, or a lone accent mark shown first, which is all Chromium on Linux gives (its keys arrive as `Process`). Any other composition, even one that commits a single Latin letter (Pinyin, Telex), is refused as IME text, as is a preview or commit no Latin keyboard key produces (another script's letter, digit or punctuation, CJK, kana, full-width, an emoji, more than a mark and a key; a Latin layout's own symbols, such as ’ — ⁴, still type) and a change to letters already typed such as the press-and-hold accent menu. Every key of a commit is scored before the passage can end; one carried past the last letter (`"h` on a closing `"`) is a mistake against that letter, is not kept, and shows as extra in the result's character breakdown, while one that is that letter (`""`) adds nothing; a Windows mark sent as an input of its own waits for the next key's input, so the pair is scored together there too. A held accent key's repeats are ignored like any key repeat (including the `Process` repeats Chromium on Linux sends), as is the letter that commits a mark it left pending, while another accent key pressed during the hold, or after the key is let go out of the field (focus elsewhere, a finished run), types as usual; a mark still pending when focus leaves is dropped; custom text takes accent keys like any other passage, and a commit made before a timed run's limit is scored even when its key arrives after the limit; interrupted attempts are never stored as comparable results. All motion scales to zero under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Keybindings

Settings contain `.keybindings-category` and `#keybindings-form`, with labelled text fields `#keybinding-restart`, `#keybinding-commands` and `#keybinding-exitFocus`, recording buttons, inline validation errors and `#keybindings-status`. Save validates and applies the entire set; reset restores defaults. `src/keybindings.js` owns parsing, conflicts, migration and matching. Bindings live in profile preferences under `keybindings`; legacy `restartKey` migrates once. Signed-in preferences use the existing cache/cloud path; guest preferences stay in memory. Hints and `aria-keyshortcuts` reflect saved bindings. Dialogs, menus, settings and ordinary editable fields take priority over restart and exit-focus shortcuts. Commands can open from menu controls, closing the menus; binding recording and ordinary editable fields suppress this shortcut. Exact modifiers are required; IME, AltGr and repeat events do not trigger actions. Restart applies to solo input/results and the local race input; online rooms cannot restart. Native Enter/Space button activation and focus traversal are preserved.

`#import-trigger`, the last row of settings (and the palette command "import Monkeytype settings…"), opens `dialog#import-dialog`. `form#import-form` holds `#import-text` (the pasted export), `#import-choose` (opens the hidden `#import-file`, whose text fills the field), `#import-error` and `#confirm-import`, disabled while the field is empty. `src/monkeytype.js` parses and maps the export; matching settings are applied as picks (`saveChoice`), and a change of test conditions starts a new practice run. `#import-report` then replaces the form: `#import-summary` (how many settings, and whether they sync or last only for a guest's visit), `#import-mapped` and, when anything was left as it was, `#import-skipped-group` with `#import-skipped`. Each row names the Stilltype setting, the value or the reason, and the Monkeytype key it came from (`.import-from`). Custom lengths, the correction rule, strict space, quick end, minimums, repeat quotes, the keymap layout, typing sounds and sound volume are among the settings mapped (see `src/monkeytype.js` for the rules). Opening it mid-run marks the run interrupted, like the palette.
