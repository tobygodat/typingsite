# 0014. Quick match, a race rating, and a trace check before rating

Status: Accepted, 2026-11-10 (TOB-421).

## Context

A race needed a friend who was online and an invitation, so someone with no friends on Stilltype had nobody to race and nothing to climb. `PRODUCT.md` deferred public matchmaking and ranking until integrity work came first, and keeps three separate scores: skill progress, quote difficulty and a race rating.

## Decision

**Pairing.** A signed-in player presses Quick match and asks the service about once a second (`public.quick_match`, `queue`). The request pairs the asker with the waiting player whose rating is closest, within a gap of 100 points plus 10 for every second the longer of the two has waited. Two players at the same rating pair at once. Two players 400 apart pair after 30 seconds. Within the gap, anyone else comes before the racer either of the two last raced (`race_private.last_opponent`, `pickOpponent`), then the closest rating. Every queue request holds one transaction lock, so two requests never pair at once. `race_private.match_queue` holds one row per account (its primary key). Nobody is paired with themselves, paired twice, or queued while seated in a live quick match. A racer who left a race that is still on stays seated in it until it settles, so their queue request waits rather than pairs. A player not heard from for 10 seconds leaves the queue.

**The room.** A pair becomes an ordinary two-player room (`rooms.quick`), counting down at once, so the shared countdown, live progress, results, review, rematch and tally all work as they already do. The standard setup is fixed: a 30-second timed race on the 200 most common English words, with no punctuation or numbers, under stop on error. The service draws the passage itself, so neither racer picks the text: the same 200 words as the browser's list (a unit test reads both), single spaces, none at either end, cut at a word's end to 991 to 1000 characters. That is past the 875 characters a run at the 350 wpm cap types in 30 seconds, so a checked run never reaches the end. A run that does finishes early, and the check finds its end off the 30-second clock (`times`). A seat counts as there once its browser asks the race about the room. A room whose other seat never came is called off at the start, so nobody races someone who isn't there. Leaving the queue calls off a room the seat never came to. That covers a "paired" reply that lands after Stop. The browser ignores such a reply, and its Stop request reaches the service after it (`queueStep` in `src/quick-match.js`).

**Nobody waiting.** After 20 seconds the control says nobody else is looking. It offers to keep waiting or to race your own fastest recorded run on the standard setup with the ghost caret, labelled as your own recording. Only the device's own recording is used. Other people's recordings stay on their devices ([ADR 0006](0006-recordings-stay-on-the-device.md)).

**Rating.** We use Elo, because one number per account that a reader can check by hand fits a site this size. Glicko's rating deviation is more than it needs. Everyone starts at 1500. K is 40 for an account's first 10 rated races, so a new rating finds its level fast, and 20 after that. The rating is called provisional below 10 rated races and is always shown with its count. A win scores 1, a tie 0.5 and a loss 0. The change is K × (score − expected), rounded half away from zero, from the two ratings before the race, so two racers at the same K move by equal and opposite amounts. Only a finished quick match moves the rating.

**Repeat pairings.** Between the same two accounts only the first three rated results in any rolling 24 hours move ratings (`race_private.rate`, decided once per room under both ratings' row locks; `REPEAT` in `src/quick-match.js`). After that the race still runs and shows its result, cards and tally included, and both racers read "Not rated: you and Lin have raced each other too often today." A decided result that isn't rated is always this case. Practice, the daily, ghost races, invited races and rematches never do. The rating touches no best, trend, training evidence or quote difficulty.

**Who decides.** The service decides the result from the two keystroke traces, as ghost races and the tally already do. It writes each seat's row in `race_private.rated_results` once. Calling it again for that seat changes nothing. That row is the one record of who won: the winner mark on both result cards, the head-to-head tally and the rating line all read it. A run that couldn't be checked is never marked the winner; its card shows its speed muted, with "not checked" (or "left") in place of its accuracy. A race with no checked run marks no winner and moves no tally.

**Settling a started race.** Once the shared start time has passed, nothing a racer does or fails to do on their own side avoids a loss. A seat finishes only with its own finishing packet, the one that carries its keystroke trace, and its verdict comes from that packet. Progress carries no trace and never finishes a seat or decides a verdict, whenever it lands. A seat with no finishing packet by the end of the grace counts as absent. A seat counts only with a finished run whose trace passes the check below, and **a win is rated only from the winner's own checked run**.

- Both count: the traces decide, more wpm and then more accuracy, rated.
- One counts: it wins and the other loses, rated. The winner reads "Rated win: Lin didn’t finish." or "Rated win: Lin’s run couldn’t be checked.", and the other racer reads "Rated loss: your finish didn’t arrive before the race closed." or "Rated loss: your run couldn’t be checked." with what the check found.
- Neither counts: no winner, unrated, and each racer reads why, "Not rated: neither racer finished with a run that could be checked." The race result still shows.
- Leave race after the start, before finishing, while the other racer is still in the race, is a forfeit: a rated loss for the leaver at once. The race goes on for the other racer ("Lin left the race. Finish your run to win."), who finishes the 30 seconds alone and wins, rated, only with a checked run. If they also leave, or finish a run that can't be checked, the race is unrated for them, and the leaver's loss stands. A seat that has finished, or whose rival has already left, just leaves. Once every seat has finished or left, the race settles. Leaving during the countdown calls the race off, unrated.

**The one-sided arithmetic.** The leaver's change is written when they leave: round(K_leaver × (0 − E_leaver)), E_leaver = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_stayer − R_leaver) / 400)), from the two ratings at that moment, and the leaver's rated count goes up by one. The stayer, if their run is checked, is written at the settle as round(K_stayer × (1 − E_stayer)), E_stayer = 1 − E_leaver, from the same two ratings (the leaver's is read from the leaver's own row). The leaver can't start another quick match until this one settles. Otherwise the stayer's row is unrated with a change of 0. Example, pinned in both test suites: X at 1600 with 12 rated races (K 20) leaves against Y at 1500 with none (K 40). E_X is 0.640, so X moves by −13 and Y not at all. Each row is written once under its room's row lock and both ratings' row locks, and a second call finds it and changes nothing.

**The Elo invariant.** Every rating is 1500 plus the changes in its own rows. A race both seats are rated in moves them by K × (score − expected) each, which sums to zero at the same K. A race only one seat is rated in is a forfeit's loss alone: one row, a loss, a change of zero or less. No rating rises without its own checked run in that race. `supabase/tests/quick-match.sql` checks each over the settlement table, and the model test in `tests/quick-match.test.js` replays every row in the order written.

The rule is in one place on each side, `race_private.rate` and the fake's `rate` in `e2e/fake-quick-match.js`. The settlement table in `supabase/tests/quick-match.sql` runs every case against the real SQL, and `tests/quick-match.test.js` plays the same rows through the fake and requires the same result.

**When it settles.** At once when every seat has finished or left. Otherwise ten seconds after the 30-second race clock ends, 45 seconds after the pairing. Every seat still racing is then finished on its last progress, and a seat with no checked run counts as absent. Ten seconds is the grace every race already gives the last progress (`race_private.advance`). A tab sends progress every 650 ms and retries a failed send at once, so ten seconds covers about fifteen sends and a short dropout. It is also how long the service already waits before it shows a seat as disconnected or drops a queued player. A longer grace keeps the winner waiting on a racer who has gone, and a shorter one turns a slow link's last packet into a loss.

**Which request settles it.** The first of these, each of which settles every due quick match before it reads anything:

- A request about the room from either seat. The staying racer's tab asks every 650 ms while it is open, so the win lands within a second of the grace ending.
- Any quick match request from anyone (`race_private.settle_due`). Every searching tab asks once a second, and opening the Online tab reads the rating, so a racer who closed the page and comes back sees the result first.
- Anyone opening a race room (`race_private.open_room`), before it deletes old rooms.

A race can't stay unsettled where it matters. Every rating anyone reads or is paired on comes from a quick match request or that room's own snapshot, and each runs the settle first. A room is deleted only by `open_room` or by pairing, both after the settle, and a result outlives its room. While nobody makes any request, nobody reads a rating either. The first request settles everything due. Settling is idempotent. The rows are written once under the room's row lock. `settle_due` skips a room another request holds, because that request is settling it or rolls back and leaves it for the next one. So a `rating` read in that moment can show the rating from before that settle. The page corrects itself on its next read: the racer in the room gets the result from the room's own snapshot, which the locking request writes, and its view then reads the rating again; anyone else's rating line is read again on the next queue reply (every second while searching) or the next visit to the Online tab. A replayed request, a second settle, or a finish that arrives after the seat was settled finds the rows and changes nothing, and a late finish's reply carries the settled result.

**The browser's clock.** The browser sets its race clock from the snapshot's `serverNow` when the reply arrives, so it runs behind the service's by the reply's one-way trip. The design no longer depends on that: the verdict comes from the finishing packet, whenever it lands inside the grace, and the service caps a racer's elapsed time at the race clock, so a late clock costs the racer nothing. Allowing for half the round trip would risk the opposite on an uneven link, a browser ahead of the service, whose finish before the clock the service refuses. In the browser, with 0, 50, 100, 200, 300 and 600 ms added to one racer's requests, three runs each (`flows/quick-match-latency.mjs`), both honest runs were checked and the faster racer won, rated, all 18 times. Before this, 9 of 9 runs at 0, 50 and 300 ms lost a checked run to `no-trace`.

**Trace check.** Each racer's finishing packet gets a verdict (`race_private.trace_check`, mirrored by `traceCheck`). No verdict is taken once that seat's result is written. The limits, in both places and kept equal by `tests/quick-match.test.js`:

| Verdict | Rule | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `no-trace` | A finish with no trace. | Without the keys, nothing can be checked. A reload mid-race sends none. |
| `unreadable` | Not the v1 format, over 6000 keys, keys outside printable ASCII and backspace, or an end over 10 minutes. | These are the ghost challenge limits. |
| `times` | Not one whole, non-negative time per key, steps over 999999 ms, an end before the last key, or an end off the 30-second clock. | Missing, negative or reordered times can't be a run on the race clock. |
| `intervals` | 20 keys in a row each under 20 ms after the one before, or over 100 keys a second over the run. | 20 ms is 600 wpm. Key rollover gives short pairs and triples. Twenty in a row is four words at 600 wpm, which is what a paste or a script gives. 100 keys a second is the race service's existing limit. |
| `wrong-text` | The trace doesn't replay to the correct characters the service settled. | The keys are for some other text. |
| `speed` | Over 350 wpm. | A judgment, not a measurement: it is set well above the speeds the fastest typists post on short common-word tests, so an honest run doesn't reach it. |

This is a floor, not anti-cheat. A trace is browser data, and a careful script can pass every rule here.

**What it costs an honest racer.** An honest run whose finish reaches the service within the grace passes every rule; the latency sweep above is the evidence for links up to 600 ms. A real disconnect that outlasts the grace, or closing the page, after the start is a rated loss against a checked run, as in other rated games. A reload after the start resumes the race, but the browser no longer has the keys from before the reload, so it sends no trace and that run counts only when the other doesn't either. A false positive on the check costs that racer the race. For a person each rule is out of reach:

- `no-trace` and `unreadable` describe the browser's record, not the typing. Only a finishing packet is checked, and the browser always puts its trace on that packet unless the page was reloaded mid-race. A 30-second run at 350 wpm is under 900 keys against a limit of 6000. The online race already turns an accent key into a stand-in letter and ignores a dead key's composition, so an honest record stays printable ASCII.
- `times` checks the browser's own clock. The race page stops recording at the 30-second mark and writes that end itself.
- `intervals` needs twenty gaps in a row each under 20 ms, which is 600 wpm held for four words. A person at 200 wpm averages 60 ms a key. Rollover gives a short pair or triple, and twenty in a row is what a paste or a script produces. A hundred keys a second over the run is 1200 wpm.
- `wrong-text` replays the keys against the progress the same browser sent. An honest run replays to exactly what it reported.
- `speed` is the one judgment. 350 wpm on 30 seconds of common words is set above what the fastest typists post on short tests, and it is the rule most worth revisiting if a real run ever trips it.

**What a stranger sees.** The other racer's race name (1 to 40 characters, as every race uses), the race itself, and the results. Never an email, an account id, a friend list or history. A name filled in from the account's email is sent as "Racer". The first press on a device says what the other racer sees. Quick match is opt-in by pressing it. After the race, either racer can add the other through the existing friends list, which never reveals an email or id.

## Consequences

- Every new table cascades from `auth.users` ([ADR 0008](0008-account-deletion-cascades.md)). A rated result keeps no id of the other racer, so it goes with its own account only.
- Closing the tab, dropping the connection or sending a run that fails the check after the start is a rated loss against a run that passes. Leaving after the start is a rated loss whatever the other racer does. Nobody can avoid a loss that way, and nobody wins rated without their own checked run.
- **What this leaves open.** Collusion and multiple accounts. Two accounts can still trade three rated results a day: a fresh account typing three checked runs against a fresh second account that forfeits gains about +54 (+20, +18, +16), and more second accounts multiply that. A player who farms strangers' forfeits gains no more than honest play allows, because each win needs their own checked run. Rated play doesn't need a confirmed email: `public.quick_match` checks only that the account exists. Closing this (a confirmed email, a cap on rating from one opponent across days, flagging pairs of one-sided forfeits) is TOB-425.
- Deleting an account deletes its rooms and results ([ADR 0008](0008-account-deletion-cascades.md)), so a race the deleted account was in is never rated and the other racer gets no win. The deleted rating goes with it.
- Changing a limit, K, the gap or the standard setup means a new migration and the same change in `src/quick-match.js`. The unit test fails until both agree.
- Deferred: the daily leaderboard (the next TOB-421 pull request), seasons, full anti-cheat, rating decay, and a count of players online.
